I'm starting this blog mostly because I've been building a lot of weird GTM stuff over the last few months and I don't really have a place to explain the thinking behind it.

My portfolio shows the projects.

But it doesn't really show the trenches I've been through.

I really went from what is an ICP to building whole sales agents.

The random bullcrap ideas.\
The broken workflows (A lot of them - we'll get there).\
The "wait, why are we even targeting these accounts?" moments.\
The part where something that started as a small outbound fix slowly became a full system.

That's what I want this blog to be about.

A few months ago, I was working on outbound at my new internship ([gethuntd.com](https://gethuntd.com)) and the problem looked simple from the outside.

Find companies.\
Find people.\
Enrich contacts.\
Send messages.\
Book meetings.

Well atleast that's the Fanatical Prospecting/LinkedIn version of it.

The real version was wayyy more complicated.

The account lists were noisy. The enrichment was inconsistent. A lot of the targeting logic was sitting in someone's head. And even when we had data, we still had to figure out what actually mattered.

Truth be told I was a rookie with a BOMB tied to my head but i still was annoyed and that was not the writing emails part or the scraping and enrichment part.

The annoying part was this:

How do you know who is "actually" worth going after?

So I started duct-taping things together.

A scraper here.\
A classifier there.\
A LOT of Apollo exports.\
Some enrichment logic.\
An LLM call I wasn't sure would work.\
A deduplication logic that I wrote at 1am and was too scared to touch after it started working.\
Some very and I mean VERY questionable workflows that somehow worked better than they had any right to.

At some point, I realised I was basically building a bootleg version of Clay for our own GTM motion with no actual idea about what Clay was.

Not as a product.\
Not as a clean platform.\
Just a bunch of workflows stitched together to answer one question better

Who should we target and why?

That's when GTM started becoming more interesting to me.

Because the real game was not "send more emails." or blow the volume up thats just the High Card.

Anyone can do that now with enough credits in a GTM tool.

The real game is understanding the market better than the next person. Building systems that can spot useful signals, clean messy data, connect context, and turn all of that into a GTM motion that actually makes sense and this is the Royal Flush.

That's the part I vibed with.

The weird middle between engineering and sales - a happy purgatory of sorts.

Writing code where the output is not an app, but pipeline.

Using AI not just to generate copy, but to make better decisions before a campaign even starts.

Building workflows that make a small team feel much bigger than it is.

So yeah, this blog is probably going to be a mix of things.

Some build logs.\
Some GTM thoughts.\
Some notes on AI and sales.\
Some things I broke.\
Some things I'm still figuring out.

I'm not writing this as an expert.

I'm writing it because I'm early, curious, and slightly obsessed with this space.

And because I'm certain the future of GTM is going to belong to people who don't just know how to use tools, but know how to build the workflows behind them.

This is me documenting that.
